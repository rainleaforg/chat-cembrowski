BAPa stands for **Biological, Analytical and Pre-analytical variation** — the three sources of
variation in a clinical laboratory result that BAPa's methods are built to separate and quantify.

BAPa is both a research program and a free, browser-based analysis tool. The research, led by
Dr. George Cembrowski, develops statistical methods (Sequential Difference Deviation, or SDD) for
validating clinical laboratory quality using patients' own paired results rather than external
control material. The tool makes those methods usable directly: a lab uploads its own result data
and gets an SDD analysis back, with the calculation running entirely in the browser rather than on
a server.

Jenna Rimkus, an undergraduate Geomatics Engineering student at the University of Calgary,
co-created BAPa with Dr. Cembrowski — her research on biological, analytical, and pre-analytical
variation, combined with software development, is what turned the underlying methodology into the
tool itself.

Some of the published research behind BAPa refers to this same work under an earlier name, PAAN.
That name has been retired; BAPa is the current and correct name for the program and the tool.

BAPa is not a diagnosis or treatment tool. It analyzes result variation for laboratory quality
purposes, and does not interpret results for individual patient care.
